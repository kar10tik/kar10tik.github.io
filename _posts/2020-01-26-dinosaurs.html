--- layout: post title: "Science v/s Religion" subtitle: "In in a nutshell." date: 2020-01-26 23:45:13 -0400 background: '/img/posts/01.jpg' --- Science v/s Religion in a nutshell

This is an undated piece from my early teens. Although it doesn't reflect my current opinion on the science-religion divide, I found it useful.

Living in the crowded streets of India, I, as a teen, am not very-well acquainted with the extravaganza of the local culture. The pompous religious festivities and rituals never had soothed my thinking: how could such a large population, well learned and educated, still believe on unproven and unsubstantiated conceptions, developed over millennia ago? Evolution changed everything: from politics and economy to lifestyles and culture; only was the 'mighty' religion left untouched. Why weren't such beliefs abolished with the onset of logical and practical mindsets? Nevertheless, we still worship age-old superpowers as "gods and 'goddesses", while being trapped in webs of blind faith and businesses. I, myself, am today baffled at times when I'm coerced to take up a lighted candle in front of a black stone, and to believe blindly on its existence, all without any proof! When everything exists today with logical and practical theories, why do we have such psychological ailments? Well, it's all (hi-)story, and we again need to turn our heads back to a yore of human existence.

Religion is the largest pandemic I ever saw, not arising with bacteria or any other germ, but of a single idea that struck in a unique mindset in one of the oldest civilisations. And it spread so fast: not even malaria had killed so many people in the past than religion ever trapped humans. Surprisingly began the construction of shrines: from temples to mosques and Gurudwaras to churches, this pandemic had now set up a permanent position in the minds of the people. Inevitably, a growth spurted in the form of a tradition of 'worship'; seriously, people found the most effective and fastest way of gaining trust and money! But from here, the lines of progress get blurred: the biggest threat that could ever rise finally made its debut as atheism!

Contrary to the beliefs of most atheists, religion probably has been said to be developed on the complex principles of science, that were simplified in the form of such believable theories (impractical, too), best found in Hinduism. Tracing the history of the famous Angkor Wat in Cambodia, Hinduism flourished with polytheistic beliefs in its budding years; male and female superpowers both were worshipped with equal weightage. Each god performed various functions: providing money, luck, light, destruction, creation, energy, and other values that couldn't be easily achieved by simply struggling.

But now, on a high time of progression, it's the time to turn away the blind eyes towards these beliefs. For the only aspect where no transformational change ever took place in history arises a matter of scrutiny and suspicion; but this isn't easy: facing mass controversy, protest and hatred is not the mission of this writing: a simple and comprehensive logic has to be developed on the existence of religion itself.

When I usually speak to theists about why do they believe in a single superpower, there is a distinct answer every time. People call it no less than age-old trends, invisible controls, and many more. But two were very astonishing: how could one say religion is a thing which doesn't move on logic, it moves on belief? Belief is strong to attract anything in the universe, but not too strong that it could build a mass mindset which is instilled into children before they're even born!
